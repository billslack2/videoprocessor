# VP-0232: Move subtitle verification off the renderer thread

## Status

Backlog

Requested 2026-11-16. The user wants subtitle verification to be faster and removed from the renderer thread. This is a focused performance and scheduling story; VP-0225 remains the broader playback-stall investigation. Coordinate changes with VP-0214 and VP-0231 before modifying shared subtitle code. No implementation, deployment, or live acceptance is claimed here.

## User story

As a viewer, I want current subtitles to remain accurate and stable while live HDMI video advances on time, even when verification is expensive, late, unavailable, or encounters changing text and picture content.

## Evidence and scope

- VP-0231's Ben-Hur investigation found that current-pixel verification and its state updates still execute synchronously on the renderer path. Calls include `SubtitlePendingMeasurementGuard::Resolve` and `RememberAccepted`, `SubtitleCueContinuationGuard::Prepare`, `Remember` and `AdvanceVerified`, and `SubtitleBoxPresentation::RefreshReferencePixels`. Some paths sample up to 32,768 or 65,536 pixels; a live opposite-edge event logged 46,072 samples. These are work bounds, not measured worst-case frame costs.
- The stages straddle presentation: a current proof may be needed before `Consume`, while the accepted cue and its seed certificate are known only after it. Moving one function alone does not remove the other render-thread checks and can break ordering. The existing subtitle measurement worker accepts up to eight queued jobs and can reorder them, so do not assume it is a safe owner for sequential continuation state without proving the contract.
- In a short logged 24 Hz window the deployed worker did 120 full scans for 120 source frames, with approximately 17.8 ms mean and 26–27 ms peak per scan. On saved decoded-Dolby-Vision 4:2:2 Ben-Hur frames, a separate detector-only phase probe measured mean 11.153 ms across seven measured runs: about 3.743 ms source/palette/mask, 4.925 ms grouping and 2.478 ms backing/finalization. Different measurements have different scopes; neither establishes the capture-to-display critical path or 60 Hz qualification.
- A preliminary static-cue reuse prototype was rejected: it could miss added picture-side text, its result could not safely reach composition with fresh bar/ink/cleanup proof, and tightening its candidate gate did not yield reuse on the saved incident frames while increasing full-scan cost. The uncommitted experiment in `E:/codex/videoprocessor/subtitle-static-cue-reuse-20261116` is evidence, not an approved patch. The separate GPU-failure recovery prototype in `E:/codex/videoprocessor/benhur-placement-dropout-20261116` is also unapproved and does not establish safe current-source presentation.
- The local capture `C:/Videoprocessor/vp/logs/analysis-20261115-154925-19564-1` includes native frames and identity sidecars. Files 000003–000063 are consecutive source frames 2147–2207, with a gap before the run. The separate MP4 is not synchronized with that diagnostic. Preserve source identities, gaps and private pixels; do not commit footage.

## Required work

1. On the current remote beta, trace and measure the complete verification, seed, refresh and cleanup chain, including work that occurs before and after subtitle presentation. Attribute total mandatory capture/render/present work and worst-case frame costs, worker time, queue wait, source/presentation identity gaps and end-to-end latency at the actual input and display rates. Compare against released RC6 and pre-RC6 packages where subtitles worked well, identifying their actual source pins; a release label alone is not a pin.
2. Move expensive current-source pixel checks, cue/template construction and any required grouping or cleanup verification onto a separate bounded worker path. Design ordered pre- and post-presentation state transitions or an equivalent verified protocol: pin immutable current-source storage, bind results to exact frame identity, timestamps, source/format/domain, profile, viewport, renderer, policy and continuity; reject stale, failed, late, out-of-order and reset-crossing results. The renderer must never wait for optional work. A missed deadline must leave the current video eligible for its normal presentation schedule.
3. Establish a cheaper verified reuse path for unchanged cues before fresh full grouping. Reuse only text/layout/glyph templates when current pixels independently verify the glyphs, backing and possible new or changed text throughout the relevant search region. Moving movie background outside glyphs is not itself a cue change. Any ambiguous or changed evidence falls back to a bounded fresh worker decision. Never reuse old movie, background or cleanup pixels or invent subtitle absence, crop or physical-aspect authority.
4. Make missing, delayed and failed verification or subtitle preparation select an explicit pixel-preserving fallback for the eligible current source, including caption pixels that would otherwise be cropped. Preserve source identity and timestamps, normal cadence conversion and deinterlacing; never hold or replay a previous whole video frame to wait for an optional decision. Keep fallback presentation distinct from native picture detection and do not force a screen or standard aspect. Coordinate with VP-0225 rather than independently duplicating its broader recovery work.
5. Record a source-pinned, reviewable implementation and evidence. Reuse validated previous work only after checking its exact source, scope, real-time scheduling and regression results. Do not increase queue depth, lookahead, timeouts or confirmation delays to conceal stalls. Any requested deployment must use a successfully completed x64 Release build and preserve active user configuration.

## Acceptance and validation

- On real captured consecutive sources, report baseline and candidate full-scan/fast-reuse counts, worker and render critical-path mean, p95 and worst case, queue age, latency and source/presentation gaps. Demonstrate that an unchanged multi-second cue takes the cheaper verified path before full grouping, while a changed or new line promptly triggers a fresh decision. Show that total mandatory work meets the applicable frame budget, not merely average latency or renderer drop counters.
- Exercise worker saturation, slow/missing/failed/out-of-order results, source and timestamp discontinuities, profile/format/viewport/renderer resets and shutdown. Prove distinct eligible current-source video continues, with original source identity and timestamps, bounded latency and no growing backlog. Include fault injection through actual submission and distinguish genuine device failure or required cadence/deinterlacing from an optional-decision veto.
- Verify current caption and picture pixels through the worker, cue tracker, placement, cleanup, composition and submission—not just a detection flag. Cover dense/changing and multiline captions, new text beside or inside an old box and on the picture, different colors and low contrast, static cues over moving background, backing changes, cue ends, volume/player UI and persistent overlays. Reject stale/mismatched proof and preserve the original caption when composition cannot be safely prepared.
- Include Ben-Hur's low-contrast edge incident, genuine picture/AR transitions, dark/full-black scenes, asymmetric bars and Off-mode framing stability. Preserve the independently verified fixes in VP-0214 and VP-0231 and keep AR detection independent of display fit. Run relevant native, worker and actual renderer regressions, including both shipped renderer dependencies when applicable; report unavailable live HDMI/scanout testing and do not claim success from still images alone.
